📊 What to Look For

✅ Buying Tips

  • Know your core need: running, swimming, sleep, or general health?
  • Battery life matters — devices that need frequent charging often end up in a drawer
  • GPS accuracy varies widely — runners should go with Garmin or Coros
  • Match your phone ecosystem: Apple Watch only works with iPhone
  • 24/7 comfort matters more than any spec sheet feature

❌ Common Misconceptions

  • Don't over-rely on calorie data — all devices have a 10-20% margin of error
  • Sleep tracking is a reference, not gospel — don't stress over the data
  • More sensors don't always mean better accuracy
  • Don't overlook the app experience — a clunky app will kill your motivation

💡 FAQ

Q: What's the difference between a fitness tracker and a smartwatch?

The line is getting blurrier. Generally speaking, fitness trackers are lighter with longer battery life and a stronger focus on health and activity. Smartwatches have bigger screens, support third-party apps, and function more like a phone on your wrist. This list covers both types — choose based on your priorities.

Q: How accurate is wrist-based heart rate monitoring?

Optical wrist-based heart rate sensors perform well during daily use and most workouts except high-intensity interval training. If you need maximum accuracy (e.g., for pro training), we recommend pairing with a chest strap.
